# Prompt Pattern

Use this reference when generating or revising an illustration.

## Generation prompt

```text
Use case: flat botanical hero illustration OR text-free botanical education poster

Input roles:
- Image 1: authoritative SUBJECT reference for morphology and growth structure.
- Image 1 is not a color reference; discard its photographed local colors after structural analysis.
- Image 2: STYLE reference only for abstraction, palette logic, line character, texture, and decorative rhythm.
- Additional botanical images, if present: references for missing organs, seasons, or life stages; do not replace the hero subject.

Output mode:
- [hero illustration / botanical education poster]
- Keep [supplied subject view] as the largest hero image.
- For poster mode, add [3–5 distinct organ or life-stage studies] and communicate relationships visually without labels.

Recognition contract:
- [distinctive silhouette]
- [lobe/wing/petal/leaflet count or arrangement]
- [attachment and branching relationship]
- [cluster, spacing, and orientation pattern]
- [distinctive seam, margin, vein, or negative space]

Structural reconstruction:
- Preserve [main gesture and key branches].
- Keep [meaningful overlaps or clusters].
- Remove [background/pot/incidental elements].
- Use intentional negative space without regularizing natural asymmetry.

Botanical education modules (poster mode only):
- Hero: [whole plant / branch / flower cluster / fruit cluster].
- Supporting study 1: [leaf or compound-leaf structure].
- Supporting study 2: [flower and bud / inflorescence detail].
- Supporting study 3: [immature and mature fruit or pod].
- Supporting study 4: [seed / opened fruit / seasonal stage], only if reliable.
- Arrange asymmetrically with 35–50% calm negative space; vary scale and cropping; avoid a rigid grid.
- No text, labels, letters, captions, signatures, or watermarks when a text-free result is requested.

Creative concept:
- One-sentence idea: [plant trait] becomes/interacts with [playful metaphor or tiny scene].
- Dominant transformation: [enlarge/open/slice/nest/repeat/turn into a stage or vessel].
- Motion device: [ribbon/orbit/path/hanging line/frame].
- Supporting motifs: [up to two families derived from the subject or concept].
- Keep invented props visually separate from anatomical structures.

Linework-first construction:
- Draw the subject as an independent fine hand-inked line illustration before adding color.
- Outer contours are fine with subtle pressure variation; structural lines are thinner; decorative marks are hairline.
- Use slight wobble, tapered ends, uneven curves, occasional open contours, tiny gaps, overshoots, and a few doubled correction strokes.
- Vary repeated scallops, petals, veins, stamens, stars, and ribbon curves; avoid cloned geometry.
- Lines use deep softened values from the two selected hue families, not pure black.
- The subject and concept must remain readable with fills mentally removed.

Flat rendering:
- Place solid matte color shapes behind the linework.
- Allow subtle registration shifts, narrow unfilled slivers, and selected color regions that stop just short of the contour.
- Use linework and shape adjacency together to explain structure.
- No gradients, shading, highlights, shadows, translucency, or modeled folds.

Decoration:
- Echo real [seams/veins/margins/cluster rhythm].
- Add selected flat patterns or narrative props where they support the one-sentence concept.
- Maintain a clear hierarchy: plant, transformation, accents.

Palette and texture:
- Deliberately ignore the source photograph's flower, leaf, stem, sky, soil, and lighting colors.
- Choose the candy palette before mapping colors to organs. Color follows graphic role rather than botanical local color.
- The two main families may be complementary, analogous, near-complementary, or non-complementary; choose by hierarchy and graphic effect, not by a fixed color-wheel rule.
- Any hue may map to any organ: green flowers, yellow leaves, blue stems, violet buds, and similarly unnatural assignments are allowed.
- Two main hue families: [dominant family with value/saturation steps] + [supporting family with value/saturation steps].
- Dominant family covers [main organ/focal transformation]; supporting family covers [background/structure/motion device].
- Optional third-hue accent is limited to small focal points and normally about 5% or less of the image.
- Background is [quiet value and hue chosen for contrast and whitespace]; it may be neutral or a restrained hue, is not required to be blue, and must not become a competing color mass.
- White or near-white may separate shapes; outlines use a deep shade of one chosen family.
- Remap every original local color into the invented two-family system; source-color preservation is allowed only by intentional coincidence.
- Optional flat checks, dots, or stripes use only the two chosen hue families.
- Very light uniform print or paper texture only.

Avoid:
- [specific likely misreadings]
- anatomy borrowed from the style reference
- generic ornamental motifs that change organ identity
- copying the reference's specific characters, props, text, composition, or narrative
- specimen-like presentation with no dominant creative transformation
- three or more competing large hue families
- a saturated background that competes with the botanical modules
- equal visual weight for both hue families
- the source photograph's palette merely made brighter or more saturated
- automatic pink-flower/green-leaf/blue-background or equivalent natural-color mapping
- treating complementary colors as mandatory or forbidding unusual organ-color assignments
- vector-perfect Bézier curves
- uniform outline weight around every shape
- mechanically centered veins or evenly spaced radii
- cloned scallops, stars, petals, stamens, or decorative marks
- color fills that perfectly replace or hide the drawing
- text, signatures, watermarks, or unwanted containers
```

## Revision rule

Diagnose the earliest failed layer and change only that layer when possible:

- wrong identity → correct outer silhouette, lobe count, attachment, and clustering;
- too dimensional → merge light/dark regions into uniform color families;
- too vector-like → thin the contours, vary stroke weight, add controlled wobble and a few open or corrected marks;
- linework disappears under color → simplify fills, add white gaps, and restore independent structural lines;
- linework feels randomly messy → keep irregularity local and purposeful; preserve clear silhouette and attachment points;
- repeated motifs feel generated → redraw each with small changes in angle, proportion, spacing, and endpoint;
- too plain → add motifs derived from real seams, veins, margins, or growth rhythm;
- recognizable but conventional → strengthen one dominant metaphor, scale shift, cutaway, or narrative interaction;
- background feels templated → choose a new neutral or restrained hue from the subject's contrast needs; do not default to blue or warm cream;
- three colors compete → shrink the accent area or remap the weakest large area into one of the two main families;
- palette feels flat → vary value and saturation within each family instead of adding another hue;
- visually noisy → remove motif families until plant, transformation, and accents read in that order;
- too crowded → adjust spacing while preserving attachment points and cluster logic;
- poster feels like a specimen sheet → break the grid, vary scale and crop, and restore 35–50% negative space;
- supporting studies feel decorative → replace repeats with distinct organs or life stages that add information;
- too generic → restore irregularities and orientation variants found in the photo.

## Final review

- Morphology remains recognizable at thumbnail size.
- Subject and style roles have not been swapped.
- Flat color regions do not simulate illumination.
- Linework alone carries recognizable silhouette, anatomy, and motion.
- Outer, structural, and decorative strokes have a visible hierarchy.
- Curves and repeated motifs show controlled hand variation without becoming noisy.
- Fills sit behind the drawing and may show subtle registration looseness.
- The concept is describable in one sentence and visible at thumbnail size.
- Every large botanical area belongs to one of two main hue families.
- The palette is chromatically independent from the source photograph and reads as intentional candy color.
- One family clearly dominates and the second supports it.
- Any third hue remains a small accent, normally about 5% or less.
- Background supports contrast and whitespace and does not become another dominant hue.
- Invented decoration can be removed without changing the plant's identity.
- Branch topology and attachment points remain botanically plausible.
- No pot or background object remains unless requested.
- In poster mode, the supplied subject remains the hero and supporting modules cover distinct organs or life stages.
- Text-free posters communicate through visual comparison alone.
